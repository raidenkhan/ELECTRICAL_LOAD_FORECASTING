01 / The Data 70k hours from Ghana's national grid
Understanding the Dataset
What the Data Tells Us About
Ghana's Growing Grid.
Here's What We Found
Demand grew 2.5× in 8 years. From ~1,467 MW to ~3,750 MW. That's 12.4% annual growth. Compare this to Europe: 0–1%. A model trained on 2021 data won't work on 2026 data. The distribution has shifted.
Accuracy has a price tag. Every MW of forecast error costs GridCo money — either fuel burned for over-generation that nobody uses, or expensive peaker plants scrambled last-minute for under-generation. A more accurate forecast means leaner dispatch, less waste, lower costs.
What we don't know: Electrification (67% → 83% rural access), GDP, urbanisation — these drive demand but we don't track them as features. The model absorbs them blindly.
Features: demand_mw · temperature_c · hour · date · 3.95% filtered for sensor outages
Grid monitoring baseline: These same patterns define "normal" for this grid. The forecast becomes the reference. When actual load diverges from it, that's a signal — equipment stress, sensor drift, or grid anomaly. The more accurate the forecast, the smaller the fault you can detect.

02 / Aims What we set out to achieve
Research Objectives
Six Aims for This
Academia–Industry Collaboration.
1. Accurate, Robust Forecasting
Develop a data-driven load forecasting model for Ghana's national grid that handles rapid demand growth (12.4% CAGR), distribution shifts, and limited feature availability — outperforming both operator heuristics and imported deep learning architectures.
2. Error Correction
Go beyond raw predictions. Study residual errors to build second-stage correctors that extract an additional 20–40% accuracy gain from autocorrelated error patterns.
3. Production Deployment
Package research into a web application deployed at GridCo's dispatch centre — replacing a 45-minute Excel workflow with a 1-minute automated process. Keep operators in control.
4. Sustainable AI
Prove that accurate grid forecasting does not require GPU infrastructure or massive energy budgets. Demonstrate that a 36K-parameter CPU-trained model can beat Transformers on both accuracy and efficiency.
5. Insights for West African Grids
Draw generalisable lessons about load forecasting in rapidly developing electricity grids where stationary data assumptions fail.
6. Grid Intelligence Platform
Establish accurate load forecasting as the foundation for fault detection (deviations from forecast = anomalies), predictive maintenance (load patterns → equipment stress scheduling), and real-time grid monitoring (continuous health baseline).

03 / Grid Intelligence Extending the forecast into operations
Vision
From Load Forecast to Full
Grid Intelligence.
Fault Detection
The forecast IS the baseline. At 68.9 MW ARD-corrected accuracy, deviations as small as ~70 MW from expected load signal an anomaly — transformer stress, line overload, sensor drift, or illegal connection.
Fold 4's 226 MW spike? That wasn't just a bad forecast — it was a grid event worth investigating. Every anomaly is a signal. The more accurate the baseline, the smaller the fault you catch.
Predictive Maintenance
Load patterns predict equipment stress. Morning ramps, evening peaks, wet vs dry season — these stress transformers, breakers, and lines in predictable ways.
An accurate forecast tells you when the next stress event is coming. Schedule maintenance before failure, not after. At 12.4% demand growth, ageing equipment fails faster. Knowing when to inspect saves downtime and money.
Grid Monitoring
A continuous health baseline from a single model. The DLinear engine outputs: 24h forecast + real-time deviation alerts + equipment stress scores + trend analytics.
One model. One baseline. Multiple intelligence layers. The load forecast isn't the end product — it's the foundation for a smarter, safer, more efficient grid.
The same accurate forecast that saves GridCo fuel — 438,000 MWh/year better dispatch — also detects faults before they cause outages, schedules maintenance before failure, and monitors the grid in real time. Accuracy isn't just about numbers. It's about knowing what "normal" looks like.

Data Exploration

What We Found First.
Patterns Jumped Out
Daily rhythm: Morning ramp. Evening peak. Overnight trough. Every day.
Weekly rhythm: Weekdays high. Weekends low. Predictable.
Seasons: Dry season ≠ wet season. Load changes with the calendar.
Growth: Upward drift across all 8 years. +12.4% CAGR in the raw data.
On temperature: It affects load — but it's slow-moving and already baked into hour and month features. Chasing temperature adds complexity without much gain — and complexity costs energy.

Most projects reach for deep learning first. Big models. GPUs. Massive energy bills. We didn't. We asked: how far can simple, efficient methods take us?

Our rule: Establish a strong simple baseline first. If classical decomposition hits 3–4% MAPE, don't jump to a transformer. Prove the marginal value of complexity — for accuracy and for the planet.

Why DLinear Won
DLinear = Decomposition Linear. It splits load into two parts:
→ Trend — gradual direction
→ Seasonal — daily/weekly rhythm

08 / Correction Learning from prediction errors
Error Analysis
Good Results — But Errors
Told a Different Story.
Errors Are Not Random
We checked the residuals — what the model got wrong. Normally a good model's errors look like noise. Ours didn't. Correlation of 0.79 between consecutive hours. If we over-predicted hour 0, we'd over-predict hour 1 too. Errors have structure. That makes them learnable.
TIDE — A Smart Filter
Zero parameters. No training. No extra energy. Dampens recent errors using a weighted average — learns how much to trust the latest mistake vs. the long-term trend.
Result: 95.5 MW (−20.9%)
Zero additional energy cost
ARD — Probabilistic Fix
Bayesian model that learns persistent bias: "DLinear always over-predicts Tuesday 19h by 15 MW." Only works with true error access (sequential mode).
Result: 68.9 MW (−40.4% sequential)
CPU-trained · negligible energy
The Critical Finding
TIDE works because it has access to recent errors. ARD without error history? Useless. The dominant signal is time-based — not weather or calendar.
Why this matters: Batch models can't use this signal. But a streaming model — correcting hour-by-hour as actuals arrive — unlocks the full 40% gain. And it does so with near-zero operational cost while saving GridCo real fuel and money on dispatch.
All methods: Baseline 115.6 · TIDE 95.5 (−21%) · SMA-7d 106 · Kalman 98 · ARD batch 115 · ARD sequential 68.9 (−40%)
TIDE = Trend-adjusted Iterative Debiasing Engine · ARD = Automatic Relevance Determination

GridCo's peaker plants cost $150–300/MWh to run. A 50 MW under-forecast mistake can cost thousands per hour in fast-ramping generation alone. DLinear cuts that error by 35–50%. At 12.4% demand growth, the cost of being wrong compounds every year.
The Annual Picture.
50 MW avg improvement × 24 hours × 365 days = 438,000 MWh/year of better dispatch decisions. The model trains for 0.003 kWh per fold and runs on any server. The operational savings — in fuel, emissions, and peaker avoidance — are millions of times larger than the cost of the forecast.
