# Electrical Load Analysis & Strategic Forecasting Report

## 1. Executive Summary
This report details the findings from a rigorous, physics-based Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) of the Load Forecasting Project dataset.
**The Bottom Line:** The dataset contains high-quality, high-frequency (15-minute) telemetry of a substation acting as both a load center and a transmission corridor.
-   **Primary Target:** "Community Load" (Sum of Transformers T1, T3, T4).
-   **Data Quality:** Exceptionally clean (100% availability), but physically complex with distinct operating regimes.
-   **Forecasting Strategy:** The load is **Stationary** but **Highly Seasonal**. A model using **Lag 96 (24h)** and **NY6ZA (Source Flow)** features must beat a baseline MAE of **11.79 MW**.

---

## 2. Phase 1: Data Truth & Physics (The Foundation)
*Objective: Verify measurements align with physical reality before modeling.*

### 2.1 Physics-Based Data Cleaning
We rejected a blind "sum everything" approach and identified the physical meaning of every signal:
-   **Community Demand (The Target):** Defined as the sum of **82T3_BANK + 82T4_BANK + 82T1_BANK**. This represents the actual electricity consumed by the district.
-   **Reverse Flow (Generation?):** **82T2_BANK** consistently shows *negative* flow (~ -12 MW). This is NOT load. It is likely a connection to a renewable source (Solar/Wind) or a back-feed to the transmission grid. **Action:** Exclude T2 from the "Demand" target; use it as an exogenous feature if it represents local generation.
-   **Transmission Corridor:** The substation imports power via **NY6ZA** (Positive Flow) and wheels it out via **AD2NY** (Negative Flow).

### 2.2 Flow Conservation
-   **Net Imbalance:** Summing all inflows and outflows yields a mean imbalance of ~88 MW (Consumed).
-   **Anomalies:** We detected **79 timestamps** where physical laws appeared to break (Imbalance > 156 MW). These indicate grid topology changes or metering errors. **Recommendation:** These specific timestamps should be masked during training to prevent model confusion.

## 3. Phase 2: Time Structure (Patterns & Drift)
*Objective: Understand how the load evolves over time.*

### 3.1 Seasonality & Drift
-   **Stationarity:** Unlike the raw line flows, the computed **Community Load is Stationary** (ADF Statistic: -10.2), meaning it reverts to a stable mean pattern.
-   **Seasonal Drift:** There is massive monthly variation. The median load swings from **~12 MW in June** (likely a major partial outage or maintenance period) to **~99 MW in August**. A tree-based model (XGBoost) trained on June will fail in August unless "Month" is explicitly encoded.

### 3.2 Weekly Patterns
-   **The "Weekend Effect" is Weak:** The difference between Weekday Mean (79.6 MW) and Weekend Mean (77.5 MW) is **less than 3%**.
-   **Intuition:** This grid likely serves 24/7 continuous industrial processes or essential infrastructure rather than a generic 9-to-5 residential suburb. "DayOfWeek" will be a weak feature.

---

## 4. Phase 3: Regime Behavior (Operating Modes)
*Objective: Identifying distinct system states.*

### 4.1 Clustering Analysis
We identified **3 Distinct Operating Regimes** using K-Means clustering on daily profiles:
-   **Regime 0 (Standard):** The dominant mode (307 days).
-   **Regime 2 (Seasonal Shift):** Secondary stable mode (196 days).
-   **Regime 1 (Transition/Chaos):** Rare mode (35 days). **Action:** Be cautious of errors on these days.

### 4.2 Event Fingerprinting
-   **High Stress Days:** 6 days exceeded **164 MW**. These are heatwaves or industrial peaks.
-   **Partial Blackouts:** 6 days had a max load < 20 MW. The substation was essentially "off". **Critical:** These days must be **removed** from training. Teaching a model that "0 MW" is a valid prediction for a Tuesday afternoon is dangerous.

---

## 5. Phase 4: Network Dependence
*Objective: Identifying causal drivers.*

### 5.1 The "Leading Indicator"
-   **NY6ZA_LINE** (The Main Incomer) has the strongest positive correlation (**0.64**) with Community Load.
-   **Lag Analysis:** Changes in NY6ZA flow often *precede* load changes, making it a valuable **Leading Indicator** feature.

### 5.2 Redundancy
-   **Dimensionality:** 96% of the information in the 11 telemetry channels can be compressed into just **6 features** (PCA components).
-   **Intuition:** The grid is highly coupled. You don't need to feed all 11 lines into the model.

---

## 6. Phase 5 & 6: Forecastability & Targets
*Objective: Setting expectations.*

### 6.1 The "Speed Limit" (Benchmarks)
We established rigid performance benchmarks. If your ML model cannot beat these, it is not adding value.
-   **Baseline (Seasonal Persistence):** Predicting "Tomorrow will be same as Today" yields an **MAE of 11.79 MW**.
-   **Volatility Risk:** The forecast is hardest between **14:00 - 16:00** (Peak Volatility > 27 MW).

### 6.2 Target Definition
-   **Envelopes:** Predicting the **Daily Peak** (MAE ~8.3 MW) is *easier* than predicting the raw 15-minute stream.
-   **Recommendation:** If the business goal is Demand Response (Peak Shaving), train a model specifically for `Daily_Max_Load` instead of `Load_t`.

---

## 7. Strategic Recommendations (Model Readiness)

### 7.1 Feature Engineering Checklist
1.  **[CRITICAL] Seasonal Lag:** `Load(t - 96)` (24 hours ago) is the single most predictive feature.
2.  **[CRITICAL] Source Flow:** `NY6ZA_LINE(t - 1)` captures incoming capacity.
3.  **[Prevent Leakage]:** Ensure all Rolling features use `closed='left'` or are effectively shifted.

### 7.2 Algorithm Selection
-   **Start With:** **SARIMAX (1, 0, 0)(1, 1, 0)[96]**.
    -   *Why?* The data is Stationary ($d=0$) but Seasonal ($D=1$). AR(1) handles immediate inertia.
-   **Upgrade To:** **LightGBM / XGBoost**.
    -   *Why?* To capture non-linear interactions (Temp * Hour).
    -   *Constraint:* Must use **Time-Series Cross-Validation** (not random shuffle) due to the Regime behavior.

### 7.3 Data Filtering
-   **Drop:** Days where `Daily_Max < 25 MW` (Outages).
-   **Mask:** The 79 timestamps with `Net_Imbalance > 156 MW` (Physics violations).
